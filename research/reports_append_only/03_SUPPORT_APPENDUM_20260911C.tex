\section*{64. The abstract obstruction is now realised in the actual geometry}
Section 61 recorded that \texttt{pairwise\_separation\_not\_sufficient} was proved against \texttt{toyRegret}, an abstract regret on $\mathsf{Finset}(\mathsf{Fin}\,2)\to\mathsf{Fin}\,3$, and that nothing showed the obstruction occurs in the support-radius model. \texttt{Lean\allowbreak Support\allowbreak Three\allowbreak World\allowbreak WitnessV1} closes that gap: three worlds with support $\{$anchor$,\ \mathrm{tip}\}$, component $j$ reading coordinate $j$, and regret given by the actual \texttt{selectedRadius}, i.e.\ half the oscillation of the omitted relations. Radii $(0,0,1)$, $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$ give zero-regret sets $\{0,1\}$, $\{1,2\}$, $\{0,2\}$: every pair intersects, the triple does not.
\claimbox{\eclass{PROVED} on \texttt{selectedRadius}, no \texttt{native\_decide}; the only two \texttt{decide} calls are on $\mathsf{Finset}(\mathsf{Fin}\,3)$. The obstruction is a property of the support geometry, not of an abstract API.}

\section*{65. Obstruction order does not determine query cost --- both directions}
Two families, deliberately opposed:
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{0.98\columnwidth}{X X}
\toprule
Family & Finding\\
\midrule
\texttt{UnboundedObstruction} & obstruction of order $m$ surviving normalisation at $\varepsilon=1/2$ (\texttt{full\_fibre\_not\_half\_safe}, \texttt{every\_proper\_subfamily\_safe}), yet \textbf{one} membership query suffices (\texttt{one\_query\_sound})\\
\texttt{CardinalityQueryLaw} & with exactly $s<m$ possible tips, the adaptive optimum is \textbf{exactly} $s$: \texttt{adaptive\_cardinality\_lower} and \texttt{adaptive\_cardinality\_attained} match\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\claimbox{\textbf{The order of the minimal obstruction is not the right cost parameter.} One family has a large obstruction and trivial cost; the other has a tight cost law tracking a different quantity. Any proposal to sell ``obstruction size determines difficulty'' as a principle is refuted by the first family --- and that principle also has prior art in the proper-learning/Helly line, so it was not available in any case.}

\section*{66. Scope and experiments}
\texttt{support\_geometry\_query\_tradeoff} and \texttt{support\_order\_vs\_queries}, plus the earlier \texttt{support\_critical\_identification} ($500$ instances at $m{=}4$, $k{=}2$, $\varepsilon{=}0.05$: $5.276$ queries against $9.21$ random, \texttt{development\_only}, \texttt{false\_safe\_count} $=0$). The comparison there is against \emph{random} only; no baseline from the decision-region literature has been run, so no claim of advantage over that line is made. Prior art remains close at the problem-definition layer, and the outstanding task is a query-complexity law for unknown feasible support with $\varepsilon$-good decisions, stated in a model those results do not already cover.
