\section*{74. The primary endpoint is now identified, and the floor held}
The confirmatory run is complete: $100$ instances $\times$ seeds $\{8201,8202,8203\} = 300$ paired instances, $\max n = 131\,072$, fixed confidence $0.95$, protocol \texttt{functional\_variance\_stopping\_v3\_weighted\_shrink\_exploration}, artifact \texttt{functional\_v3\_maxn131072\_confirm.json} (sha \texttt{7f77c8a2\dots}).

Section 70 reported $\ge 1.66\times$ against \texttt{uniform} as a \emph{floor}, because \texttt{uniform} was censored at $\max n = 65\,536$. The floor held: the measured value is $\mathbf{2.670\times}$. A conservative bound that survives its own confirmatory test is worth recording as such.

One caveat attaches to the headline number and to no other row: \texttt{uniform} clears the censoring line by only $4.33$ percentage points of certified mass ($54.33\%$ against the $50\%$ needed for an identified median), where the other five have $16$--$21$ points. The $2.670$ is therefore the figure most sensitive to the horizon --- at $\max n = 65\,536$ this same endpoint for \texttt{uniform} was the censoring sentinel, not a number.}

\section*{75. The paired structure is stronger than the medians}
Matching on \texttt{(seed, instance\_id)} across all $300$ instances:

\begin{itemize}
\item Against \texttt{uniform}: on the $163$ instances where both certify, \texttt{C\_anytime} is faster on \textbf{$163$ of $163$}. Zero losses, zero ties. Sign test $p \approx 1.7\times10^{-49}$; median paired ratio $2.183$. A further $49$ instances are certified by \texttt{C\_anytime} only and $0$ by \texttt{uniform} only.
\item Against \texttt{C\_split}: $199$ of $199$, median paired ratio $3.124$; $13$ instances certified by \texttt{C\_anytime} only, $0$ the other way.
\item Against \texttt{C\_pilot} and \texttt{C\_shrink}: \emph{not} a sweep. $193$ wins against $15$ losses in each case, median paired ratio $\approx 1.64$.
\item \texttt{false\_safe\_count} $=0$ for all six variants on all $300$ instances --- $1\,800$ runs, no false safe certificate.
\end{itemize}

The distinction in the third bullet is the one that matters for how the result may be stated. Over \texttt{uniform} and \texttt{C\_split} the ordering is \emph{domination} on the common support; over the pilot family it is a median statement with a measured $15/208$ loss rate, and must not be written as domination.

\section*{76. What it costs not to know $\sigma$, and two boundaries}
\textbf{The certifiable set is identical.} \texttt{C\_anytime} and the known-$\sigma$ oracle certify \emph{exactly the same $212$ instances} --- set equality, not merely equal counts ($|A \setminus B| = |B \setminus A| = 0$). On this protocol, not knowing $\sigma$ costs nothing in \emph{which} instances become certifiable.

\textbf{It costs a small factor in samples, and Section 70 quoted the friendlier half of it.} On medians, $34\,788$ against $34\,532$: $0.74\%$. Instance by instance the oracle is faster on $179$ of $212$, \texttt{C\_anytime} on $32$, one tie, with median paired ratio $0.9723$ --- about $2.8\%$. Both numbers belong in the ledger; the median alone understates the gap by a factor of roughly four.

\textbf{Certifiability is not a gap threshold.} $86$ of $300$ instances are certified by no variant at $131\,072$. The gap distributions separate strongly --- median true Top-$K$ gap $0.059$ for the never-certified against $0.297$ for the certified --- but they \emph{overlap}: $38$ never-certified instances have gaps above the smallest certified gap, and $20$ certified instances have gaps below the largest never-certified gap. Any future claim that certifiability is determined by a gap cutoff is already contradicted by this run.

\textbf{The horizon comparison is not available.} The $65\,536$ probe used seed $8301$; this run used seeds $8201$--$8203$. The apparent lift of $+11.7$ points for \texttt{C\_anytime} and $+17.3$ for \texttt{uniform} therefore confounds horizon with instance draw and is \emph{not} evidence of a horizon effect. Establishing one requires re-running the probe seed at the larger horizon.
\claimbox{Recorded against a specific temptation. Regressing $\log N$ on $\log(\text{gap})$ over the $212$ certified \texttt{C\_anytime} instances gives slope $-1.572$ with $R^2 = 0.805$, shallower than the $-2$ of the textbook rate. This is \emph{not} evidence against a $\text{gap}^{-2}$ law: censoring removes precisely the small-gap, large-$N$ corner, which biases the fitted slope toward zero. It is a measurement of the certified subset, not of the scaling.}
