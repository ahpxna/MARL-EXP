\section*{103. The refutation is unconditional: the witness is a binary world}
The violating fibre has $\lambda_i=(0,1,0)$, hence $\phi_i(a_i)=[a_i=1]$. Every basis column of \texttt{closed\_form\_raw\_basis\_exact} is a product $\prod_{i\in S}\phi_i(a_i)$, so every column -- and therefore every world in the fibre -- is a function of the binary key
\[
\kappa:a\longmapsto\bigl([a_i=1]\bigr)_{i=1}^{m}
\]
alone. Symbol $2$ is an exact duplicate of symbol $0$. The witness takes $64$ distinct values, not $729$.

Restricting to $\{0,1\}^6$ and evaluating in the $K=2$ model reproduces \emph{every} quantity exactly: $\delta_{\square}=4674203777/7793262048$, selected $\{0,4,5\}$, $2D=192722413097/64236637920$, margin $985/55328$, and the ratio to the last digit.

\[
\boxed{2D\le(m-1)\delta_{\square}}
\]
on the plain binary cube with corner reference, with no hypothesis at all. \textbf{D6-prime dies as a corollary, not as the primary finding.}

\textbf{Non-claim.} This does not touch the proved raw-route corpus. \section*{106. New instrument: exact rational LP ascent}
\subsection*{106.1 The functional is piecewise linear}
On the binary cube with corner reference at the all-zero point, write $\rho_i(z)=F(z[i\!\leftarrow\!1])-F(z[i\!\leftarrow\!0])$ for the discrete derivative, $c_i=F(e_i)-F(0)$ for the corner-referenced additive component, and
\[
G_S(a)=F(a)-\sum_{i\in S,\;a_i=1}c_i .
\]
Then
\[
\delta_{\square}=\max_i\Bigl(\max_z\rho_i(z)-\min_z\rho_i(z)\Bigr),
\]
\[
\operatorname{osc}_i=|c_i|,\qquad
\ell_T=\tfrac12\Bigl(\max_a G_T(a)-\min_a G_T(a)\Bigr),
\]
$S=$ the top $k=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ coordinates by $\operatorname{osc}_i$, and $2D=2(\ell_S-\min_T\ell_T)$. \emph{Every one of these is a maximum or a minimum of linear forms in $F$.}

\subsection*{106.2 Fixing the cell turns the problem into an LP}
Fix the combinatorial pattern: which $z$ attains each derivative extreme, which coordinate attains $\delta_{\square}$, the sign of each $c_i$ and the ordering of $\operatorname{osc}$ that selects $S$, the $\arg\max$ and $\arg\min$ of $G_S$ and of the competitor $G_{T^\ast}$, and which $T^\ast$ is cheapest. On the resulting polyhedral cell both $2D$ and $\delta_{\square}$ are linear. Because the ratio is invariant under positive scaling and under adding a constant, one may gauge-fix $F(0)=0$ and normalise $\delta_{\square}=1$; the linear-fractional problem becomes a genuine \textbf{linear program}
\[
\max\ \bigl(G_S(a^+_S)-G_S(a^-_S)\bigr)-\bigl(G_{T^\ast}(a^+_{T^\ast})-G_{T^\ast}(a^-_{T^\ast})\bigr)
\]
subject to, writing $z^\pm_i$ for the recorded derivative extremes:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\rho_i(z)\le\rho_i(z^+_i)$ and $\rho_i(z^-_i)\le\rho_i(z)$ for all $i,z$;
\item $\rho_i(z^+_i)-\rho_i(z^-_i)\le1$ for all $i$, with equality at the attaining coordinate;
\item $\varepsilon_ic_i\ge0$ for the recorded signs $\varepsilon_i$, and $\varepsilon_ic_i-\varepsilon_jc_j\ge\mu$ for $i\in S$, $j\notin S$, where $\mu$ is the imposed StrictTopC margin;
\item $G_T(a)\le G_T(a^+_T)$ and $G_T(a^-_T)\le G_T(a)$ for all $a$, for $T\in\{S,T^\ast\}$;
\item $G_{T^\ast}(a^+_{T^\ast})-G_{T^\ast}(a^-_{T^\ast})\le G_T(a^+_T)-G_T(a^-_T)$ for every $T$, which forces $\ell_{T^\ast}\le\ell_T$ since $\ell_T$ dominates any recorded difference.
\end{itemize}
Every coefficient lies in $\{0,\pm1,\pm2\}$, so by Cramer a vertex is rational with a \emph{small} denominator. That is the point: the search route produces $48$-digit denominators that \texttt{norm\_num} cannot touch.

\subsection*{106.3 Exactness discipline}
The float LP fixes the combinatorics; the vertex is then recovered exactly over $\mathbb{Q}$ from the active set by fraction-free elimination, and an independent exact evaluator decides whether to accept it. \textbf{The LP only ever proposes a point.} A wrong or degenerate cell therefore costs one iteration and never a false certificate. Before anything else, the evaluator was acid-tested: it reproduces the Section 102 witness bit for bit, including $\delta_{\square}$, the selected set and the margin.

This is not the same as \texttt{cell\_lp\_ascent} inside the search script, which runs the same geometric idea in floating point to \emph{generate leads} for the rational snapper. What is new is that the ascent itself is exact and its fixed point is certified.

\subsection*{106.4 Two defects, both instructive}
\textbf{(i) Taking the LP vertex loses the cell.} An LP optimum sits on a vertex, where the active pattern is maximally degenerate; score ties let the Top-C tie-break choose a different $S$, and the ratio at the vertex collapses even though the LP objective grew. Measured: predicted $0.6533$, actual $0.0000$, selected flipping from $\{2,3\}$ to $\{0,1\}$. The mitigation was already documented in the comment of \texttt{cell\_lp\_ascent} and had not been implemented here; the fix is an \emph{exact} line search on the segment from the incumbent to the vertex.

\textbf{(ii) Accepting any improvement walks onto the tie boundary.} With the line search in place but the strictness unchecked, the ascent converges to $2(k-1)/(m-1)$: $2/3$ at $m=4$ and $4/5$ at $m=6$, with relative margin $\sim10^{-17}$. That is the same attractor at exactly $0.8$ at which the superseded harness \texttt{run\_d6\_fiber\_ab\_search.py} had plateaued. The fix is to apply the StrictTopC gate to \emph{every exact candidate}, not only to the LP constraints.

\subsection*{106.5 Performance}
Seeded at the Section 102 witness, the ascent climbs $1.000440\to1.065131$ in $17.4$\,s. The stochastic search needed $1658$\,s to reach $1.000440$ and, unseeded on the same space, reaches only $0.762$.

\subsection*{107.1 Structure of the extremum}
At $\mu=10^{-5}$ the near-extremal world has $\delta_{\square}=1$ with \emph{every} coordinate derivative spread equal to $1$; the competitor is exactly the complement, $T^\ast=S^{c}$; and all $64$ values converge to integers in $\{-1,0,1,2,3\}$ with
\[
\ell_S=\tfrac72,\qquad \ell_{S^c}=\tfrac12,\qquad 2D=6=m .
\]
Writing $S=\{0,4,5\}$ and $S^c=\{1,2,3\}$, the limit depends almost entirely on $t=a\cap S^c$: with $u=[1\in t]$, $v=[2\in t]$, $w=[3\in t]$ its multilinear form is
\[
u+v-w+uv-uvw ,
\]
corrected by $[\,a\cap S\ne\emptyset\,]$ on the columns $t\in\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{2\}\}$ and by $[\,0\in a\,]$ on $t=\{1,2,3\}$. This is \emph{one vertex of the optimal face}, not a canonical formula, which is why it was not generalised directly; see Section 108.

\subsection*{107.2 The supremum is not attained}
Rounding the near-extremal world to its integer limit destroys it: the integer point is a Top-C \emph{tie}, the selection flips, and the ratio collapses to $0.4$. Every rounding to a common denominator $\le1024$ reproduces $0.4$. So $6/5$ is a supremum approached from just off a tie point and \emph{not attained}, and any witness must retain a perturbation. This is a genuine discontinuity of the functional at the tie locus, not a numerical artifact.

\section*{108. Failed programme: transferring the structure to other $m$}
\textbf{The plan.} Section 107.1 identifies where the extremum lives: infinitesimally off a Top-C tie point. Since the unseeded ascent reliably \emph{falls onto} tie points, the natural programme was two-phase seeding -- run the ascent with the gate off to land on a tie point, then perturb and ascend strictly -- giving seeds at every $m$ and so a test of $\sup=m$ beyond $m=6$.

\textbf{Diagnosis.} The obstruction is not \emph{locating} the tie point, it is the \emph{direction} of departure from it. The extremal directions form a measure-zero set among tangent directions, for the same reason isotropic sampling of worlds fails (Section 98.2). Sampling directions is the same mistake one level down.

\textbf{Consequence, stated plainly.} The exact LP ascent is a \emph{local} method. It climbs excellently when seeded inside the basin and does not find the basin. Testing $\sup=m$ at $m=4$ and $m=8$ is therefore blocked on \emph{seeding}, not on compute; more denominators, more starts and more wall-clock will not help, and should not be spent.

\textbf{What would unblock it.} Solve for the direction instead of sampling it. At a tie point the limiting ratio along a direction $d$ is a ratio of directional derivatives, and the Top-C selection is decided by the sign pattern of $d$ on the tied scores. Maximising that limit over $d$ on the tangent cone is again a linear-fractional problem, hence an LP over a finite set of sign patterns. That is a construction rather than a search, and it would produce seeds at every $m$. Not attempted.
