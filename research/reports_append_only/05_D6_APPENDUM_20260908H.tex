\section*{147. The block-composition identities hold}
For independent blocks $F(x)=\sum_b F_b(x_b)$ with the corresponding product reference, the proposed identities are
\[\delta_\square(F)=\max_b \delta_\square(F_b),\qquad 2D(F)=\sum_b 2D(F_b),\]
the second because the retained residual splits across blocks and oscillation is additive on a Cartesian product. Checked mechanically by composing two copies of the exact $m=4$ witness of Section 134 into an $m=8$ world, with $S$ the union of the blocks' selected sets:
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{0.98\columnwidth}{X r r}
\toprule
 & predicted & measured\\
\midrule
$\delta_\square$ & $\max(1,1)=1$ & $1$\\
$2D$ & $3+3=6$ & $6$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Both exact over $\mathbb{Q}$. \eclass{VERIFIED NUMERICALLY, not yet formalised}

\textbf{Consequence for the frontier programme.} Composing a $6$-block and an $8$-block at common margin $\mu$ gives $(6-3\mu)+(8-4\mu)=14-7\mu=m-\tfrac m2\mu$ at $m=14$, and two $6$-blocks give $12-6\mu$ at $m=12$. Since $\langle 6,8\rangle=\{6,8,12,14,16,\dots\}$, \textbf{$m=10$ is the one missing size} for every even $m\ge6$. The composition also confirms it is a lower-bound device only: two $m=4$ blocks give $6$, while $c^\ast(8)=8$, because the $m=4$ block value $3=m-1$ is itself suboptimal.

\section*{148. The information limit has a closed form}
Define, for an algorithm knowing the entire response profile $\mathcal I(F)=(Q_1,\dots,Q_m)$ and allowed to randomise,
\[V(\theta)=\inf_{\pi}\ \sup_{\substack{\mathcal I(F)=\theta\\ \delta_\square(F)\le1}}\ \mathbb E_{S\sim\pi}\,r_F(S),\qquad r_F(S)=L_F(S)-\min_{|T|=k}L_F(T).\]
$V>0$ says that no ranking rule, however good, removes the error: more information must be measured.

Permuting the $m-1$ coordinates of positive response slope while fixing the negative one produces worlds with \emph{identical} $Q$ and different optimal sets, with $r_F(S)=|S\setminus T_F^\star|$. On that family the game is symmetric under $S_{m-1}$, so the uniform strategy is optimal on both sides and
\[\mathbb E\,|S\setminus T^\star| = k-\frac{k^2}{m-1},\]
giving, with $m=2k$,
\[\boxed{\ V\ \ge\ \frac{k(k-1)}{2k-1}.\ }\]
Confirmed as the exact game value by linear programming over all $\binom{m-1}{k}$ strategies:
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{0.98\columnwidth}{r r r X}
\toprule
$m$ & $\binom{m-1}{k}$ & LP value & $k(k-1)/(2k-1)$\\
\midrule
$6$ & $10$ & $1.200000000$ & $6/5$\\
$8$ & $35$ & $1.714285714$ & $12/7$\\
$10$ & $126$ & $2.222222222$ & $20/9$\\
$12$ & $462$ & $2.727272727$ & $30/11$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The values $6/5$ and $12/7$ reported for $m=6,8$ are the $k=3,4$ cases of this formula.

\section*{149. NARROWING a proposed target: $m/5$ is weaker than the family already gives}
The programme proposed for this direction set as its nearest target
\begin{center}\scriptsize\oldclaim{$V\ge m/5$ at $\mu=0$, $m=6r$, using six-dimensional blocks and a global budget argument}\end{center}
Three observations, all from the table above.
\begin{itemize}
\item $k(k-1)/(2k-1)=m/5$ \emph{only} at $m=6$; it is a coincidence at that width. At $m=8$ the family gives $12/7\approx1.714$ against $m/5=1.6$, and the gap widens: the closed form is asymptotically $k/2=m/4$, not $m/5$.
\item The permutation family is defined \textbf{directly at every even $m$} --- take the $m-1$ positive-slope coordinates and let $T^\star$ range over their $k$-subsets. It needs \textbf{no block composition}, so the $m=10$ gap of Section 147 \textbf{does not apply} to this bound.
\item Consequently a composition argument for $V$ would not only be unnecessary, it would \emph{lose} the constant, since composing blocks of size $6$ recovers $m/5$ rather than $m/4$.
\end{itemize}
\claimbox{\textbf{Two programmes, not one.} (i) The Top-C frontier $R_m(\mu)=m-\tfrac m2\mu$ does need block composition and does need an $m=10$ witness. (ii) The information limit $V\ge k(k-1)/(2k-1)$ needs neither, and is already available at every even $m$. Conflating them costs the second its constant.}
