\section*{164. The chain that now exists end to end}
Reading the links in order, the following composite is closed in Lean: a legal $A$-free query design exists and its law is computed from the actual world means; a decoder over that design terminates almost surely with finite expected cost and a bounded probability of ever certifying a wrong label; labels are revalidated each epoch so that the total work spent under a wrong label has bounded expectation; that quantity converts into a bound on the \texttt{oracleRegret} contributed by those epochs; and an explicit tracker's query counts match the emitted schedule with bounded discrepancy.
\claimbox{\eclass{PROVED} for each link, with the composite assembled through \texttt{Axiom\allowbreak Audit\allowbreak Novelty\allowbreak ChainsV1} ($16$ end-to-end names). \textbf{What this is not}: regret inside correctly-labelled epochs --- learning $\mu$, tracking quality near the boundary --- and the expected stopping rate $\Gamma$ are \emph{not} closed. No matching-regret claim for unknown $A$ follows from the above.}

\section*{167. Standing scope for the whole chain}
The blind results hold for fixed linear $A$-invariant statistics of legal off queries under unit independent Gaussian noise. The discovery results hold for the stated decoder with dyadic revalidation. The matching results assume $A$ known. Discovery cost currently scales as $q\log(q/\eta)$, which forbids dropping it mechanically into the $N\asymp q$ budget regime. Track-and-Stop style asymptotic optimality has a broad existing framework, so closing $\Gamma$ at fixed $q$ completes the chain without by itself creating novelty.
