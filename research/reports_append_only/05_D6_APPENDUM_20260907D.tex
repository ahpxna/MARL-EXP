\section*{114. Five formalisation defects, recorded so they are not repeated}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\texttt{decide} over a $\Pi$ type is a machine kill, not a slowdown.} Stating a finite check as $\forall z:\mathrm{Fin}\,6\to\mathrm{Fin}\,2$ forces Lean to build the \texttt{Fintype} instance for a \emph{function} type; the kernel term is enormous. The first bridge exhausted 64\,GB of RAM on one such theorem, $6\cdot64\cdot64$ cases over a $\Pi$ type. The same facts over six explicit $\mathrm{Fin}\,2$ arguments compile in seconds. \textbf{Rule: quantify over elements, not over functions, then lift with an $\eta$ lemma.} The mixed-difference bound was restated as six lemmas of twelve explicit arguments, $4096$ integer cases each.
\item \textbf{\texttt{simp only} carries no simprocs.} After \texttt{fin\_cases} the index is $\langle k,\_\rangle$ and the conditional is a \texttt{Fin.val} equality; reducing them needs the \texttt{reduceIte} simproc and \texttt{Fin.mk\_zero}, neither of which a closed \texttt{simp only} list invokes. The linter said so, reporting the supplied \texttt{if\_true} and \texttt{if\_false} as unused. This is the \emph{second} time the \texttt{fin\_cases} numeral trap has cost this corpus a compile cycle; the first was the twelve \texttt{Fin.mk} companion lemmas of addendum B Section 92.
\item \textbf{Guessing the shape of \texttt{PolyhedralCellValid}.} Its last component compares \texttt{responseWitnessSpan}, a difference of two responses, not $\operatorname{osc}$. Three simultaneous errors had this single root. Reading the definition would have cost one \texttt{grep}.
\item \textbf{Guessing Mathlib division lemma names.} \texttt{div\_lt\_div\_of\_pos\_left} concludes $a/b<a/c$, same numerator; every goal here has the same \emph{denominator}. Replaced by two local helpers proved with \texttt{linarith}, division by a numeral being linear. Thirteen sites collapsed to \texttt{exact div17\_le \ldots}.
\item \textbf{\texttt{rw} across a cast boundary.} An integer equation cannot rewrite a real literal: \texttt{rw} looked for $(42:\mathbb{Z})$ in a term containing $(42:\mathbb{R})$. Fixed by four intermediate lemmas stating the residual extremes directly over $\mathbb{R}$.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Three of the five were the author guessing rather than reading a definition.} That is the ratio worth remembering; Lean was not the difficulty.

\section*{116. Solving for the direction, and where the derivation stops}
Addendum C Section 108 left seeding blocked: the departure direction at a tie point could not be sampled. It does not need to be sampled.

At a tie point $F_0$ set $F(t)=F_0+td$. As $t\to0^+$ every $\ell_T$ and $\delta_{\square}$ is continuous, but the Top-C \emph{selection} is not: tied coordinates are separated by the first-order term $\varepsilon_ic_i(d)$ with $\varepsilon_i=\operatorname{sign}c_i(F_0)$. Hence
\[
\lim_{t\to0^+}\frac{2D(F_0+td)}{(m-1)\delta_{\square}(F_0+td)}
=\frac{2\bigl(\ell_{S(d)}(F_0)-\min_T\ell_T(F_0)\bigr)}{(m-1)\,\delta_{\square}(F_0)},
\]
which depends on $d$ \emph{only through the selection} $S(d)$. The limit therefore takes finitely many values, enumerable exactly over the achievable top-$k$ sets. A direction realising a chosen $S$ is written down explicitly: $d(e_i)=+\varepsilon_i$ for $i\in S\cap G$, $-\varepsilon_i$ for $i\in G\setminus S$, zero elsewhere, touching only $m$ of the $2^m$ values.

\subsection*{116.2 The derivation, and its premise}
At that tie point the selected losses take $\ell_{\min}+\{0,1,\dots,k\}$ with step exactly $\delta_{\square}$, whence
\[
\max_S\frac{2(\ell_S-\ell_{\min})}{(m-1)\delta_{\square}}=\frac{2k}{m-1},\qquad k=\lfloor m/2\rfloor .
\]
For even $m$ this is $m/(m-1)>1$; for odd $m$ it is exactly $1$. That \emph{derives} the value $6/5$ which the margin sweep of Section 107 had only fitted.

\claimbox{\oldclaim{The bound $2D\le(m-1)\delta_\square$ fails if and only if $m$ is even, with sharp constant $2k/(m-1)$.}}
\textbf{Reason for demotion.} The step-$\delta_{\square}$ premise is a property of the \emph{particular} tie point, not of $m$. At $m=4$ the tie points reached from random starts have loss spectrum $\{1/2,1,3/2\}$, step $\delta_{\square}/2$, giving $\max=2/3$ rather than $4/3$. The parity statement is therefore not established, and the question moved one level again: from the direction to the choice of tie point.

\subsection*{116.3 The tie-locus LP, and why it was abandoned}
Before the face enumeration, the tie locus itself was made the feasible set: maximise the loss spread subject to $\varepsilon_ic_i=\varepsilon_jc_j$ for all pairs. That formulation is \emph{correct} -- seeded at the known $m=6$ tie point it returns LP objective exactly $6.0000$ and limit ratio $6/5$, stable across all six rounds, with spectrum $\{1/2,3/2,5/2,7/2\}$ and all oscillations equal -- but from a random start it converges to $4/5$ with spectrum $\{1,3/2,2,5/2,3\}$, step $\delta_{\square}/2$. Two further faults were identified in it and are the reason it was replaced rather than tuned:

\begin{itemize}
\item it forced \emph{all} $m-1$ tie equalities, when only the pairs straddling the $S/S^{c}$ boundary need to lie on the tie face; forcing the whole ranking spectrum cuts away most of the domain;
\item it omitted $\ell_{T^\ast}\le\ell_U$ for every $U$, so the LP optimised $\ell_S-\ell_{T^\ast}$ for a competitor that need not be the argmin. Because $\ell_{\min}\le\ell_{T^\ast}$ the reported value \emph{understates} and no false certificate is possible, but the LP lands on the wrong face.
\end{itemize}
The auxiliary-variable direction in that file was checked and is \emph{sound}: $p,q$ bound $\ell_{T^\ast}$ from above and the objective drives $p-q$ down to exactly $2\ell_{T^\ast}$. It is the opposite of the unsound pattern described in Section 118.1.

\section*{118. The face-enumeration LP}
\subsection*{118.1 Formulation}
Enumerate $(S,T)$ exhaustively -- the dimension the tie-locus LP wrongly froze to a single choice, $380$ pairs at $m=6$ -- and obtain the four extremal points by pattern iteration. Then $\delta_{\square}\le1$ is convex and needs no cell pattern: introduce $u_i,l_i$ with $l_i\le\rho_i(z)\le u_i$ and $u_i-l_i\le1$. No tie locus occurs anywhere.

\textbf{One formulation trap, recorded because it is easy to fall into.} The competitor loss must be bounded \emph{above}, by auxiliaries $p,q$ with $q\le G_T(a)\le p$ and the objective subtracting $(p-q)$; the optimiser then drives $p-q$ down to exactly $2\ell_T$. Bounding $\ell_{\min}$ from \emph{below} by a variable $y\le L_T$ and maximising $L_S-y$ is \textbf{wrong}: the optimiser pushes $y$ down without limit. That is why the competitor is enumerated rather than minimised, and why $T$ is forced to be the argmin by $p-q\le G_U(a_U^+)-G_U(a_U^-)$ at every other $U$.

\section*{120. The upper bound already exists, and it closes the picture}
\claimbox{\oldclaim{$c^\ast(m)$ will not be settled by a better search; it needs an upper bound, that is, a proof. The face family would supply one only if enumerated completely.}}
\textbf{Reason for retraction.} The proof is already in the corpus, and the author of Sections 116--119 did not look for it. \texttt{LeanD6SmallSymmetricDifference.lean} contains
\[
\ell_S-\ell_{T}\ \le\ |S\setminus T|\cdot\delta_{\square}
\]
as \texttt{pair\_loss\_le\_sdiff\_card}, under exactly the hypotheses in force here: $q$ nonnegative and normalised, $|\texttt{mixedDifference}|\le\delta_{\square}$, \texttt{IsTopKByScore} for the selected set, and $|T|=k$. Hence
\[
\boxed{\,2D\ \le\ 2|S\setminus T|\,\delta_{\square}\ \le\ 2\min(k,m-k)\,\delta_{\square}\,}
\]
and the same file already derives, as \texttt{pair\_half\_factor\_of\_small\_symmetric\_difference}, that $2|S\setminus T|\le m-1$ forces $2D\le(m-1)\delta_{\square}$: \emph{the original D6 bound}. \texttt{LeanD6SymmetricDifference\-ReductionV1} then proves, as \texttt{pair\_half\_factor\_except\_balanced\_complement}, that this covers every geometry \emph{except one}, and its docstring names it: ``an even universe split into two complementary equal halves''.

\subsection*{120.1 The counterexample sits in the only hole the reduction leaves}
The witness of Section 111 has $S=\{0,4,5\}$, $T=\{1,2,3\}$, $m=6$ even, $T=S^{c}$, $|S|=|T|=3$. That is precisely the balanced complement. \textbf{So the empirical regularity of Section 118.4 -- the winning face has $T=S^{c}$ at both $m=4$ and $m=6$ -- is not luck; it is forced.} It is the only place a counterexample can live, because everywhere else the $(m-1)$ bound is a theorem. Four instruments kept rediscovering that geometry because it is the only one available.

\subsection*{120.2 The parity statement, corrected}
For odd $m$, $k=(m-1)/2$ and $|S\setminus T|\le k$, so $2|S\setminus T|\le m-1$ \emph{always}: the hypothesis of the half-factor theorem is automatic and
\[
2D\le(m-1)\delta_{\square}\quad\text{is PROVED for every odd }m.
\]
For even $m$, $k=m/2$ and the balanced complement attains $|S\setminus T|=m/2$, giving the bound $m\,\delta_{\square}$ and no more. The earlier demoted formula $2k/(m-1)$ was numerically right and derived from the wrong object; the correct reading is
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{0.98\columnwidth}{Xcc}
\toprule
 & odd $m$ & even $m$\\
\midrule
proved upper bound on $2D/\delta_{\square}$ & $m-1$ & $m$\\
$(m-1)$ law & \textbf{PROVED} & \textbf{FALSE at $m{=}6$}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
so the law does not ``fail iff $m$ is even'' as a conjecture: for odd $m$ it is a theorem, and for even $m$ it is refuted at the one $m$ tested.

\subsection*{120.3 What is actually left}
\[
c^\ast(6)\ \le\ 6\ \ \text{(proved)},\qquad
c^\ast(6)\ \ge\ 5.99966\ldots\ \text{(measured)}
\]
the lower value being $5\times1.1932703$ from the face LP and $5\times1.1932566$ from the ascent, and the margin sweep extrapolating to $6/5$ with deficit linear in $\mu$ at coefficient $\approx6.74$. \textbf{Upper bound proved, lower bound measured to four decimal places; only sharpness -- attainment of $m\,\delta_{\square}$ in the balanced complement -- is open.} That is a far smaller gap than Sections 116--119 supposed, and it is a Lean target rather than a search target: parameterise the tie-witness perturbation family $W_t$, prove strict Top-C and the interaction bound on an interval $t>0$, show $2D/\delta_{\square}\to6$, and conjoin with \texttt{pair\_loss\_le\_sdiff\_card}.

\textbf{Consequence for the two experiments named below.} Both are now much less interesting. Asking whether any of the $380$ faces at $m=6$ exceeds $6/5$ is answered in advance: none can, because $6/5$ is the proved ceiling. And the $T=S^{c}$ regularity needs no test, being forced by the reduction theorem. \textbf{Neither should be run.} The search programme of the last two days was aimed at a question that a theorem in the same repository had already bounded.

\textbf{The state of the only open question.} What remains is the sharpness of a proved bound in one geometry at one $m$, not the location of an unknown supremum. The four instruments of Sections 116--119 retain their value as \emph{lower-bound machinery} and as a record of how a measured constant was approached, but the constant itself was never unbounded above.

\section*{121. Final status}
\textbf{Final appended status label.} As of 7 September the centred rank programme, the unconditional $(m-1)$ law, and the raw rank-one rescue are all closed in Lean 4 with clean axiom audits. What replaced them is a measurement problem with a conjectured answer $m/(m-1)$, supported at a single $m$ by two independent instruments agreeing to six digits, derived once from a tie-point spectrum whose defining property is not known to hold at any other $m$, and not bounded above by anything. The honest summary of this campaign is that it converted a false theorem into a well-posed open constant, and that every instrument built along the way was found to be weaker than its author first reported. The last correction is the sharpest: the open constant was bounded above, in this repository, before any of those instruments were written. \textbf{Search the corpus before building an instrument to measure what it may already prove.}
