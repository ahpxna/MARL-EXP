\textbf{Append-only rule.} Sections 1--53 are an immutable prefix. This addendum begins at Section 54.

\textbf{What this addendum contains.} Two instrument defects found in code that had already produced ledger artifacts, a principled repair, and a \textbf{negative headline result} that survives the repair. The negative result is the point of the addendum: the repair worked and the conclusion did not change.

\section*{54. DEMOTION: the shrink ablation was never an ablation}
Protocol \texttt{functional\_variance\_stopping\_v2\_balanced\_batches} reported
\[\textsf{cert\_frac}(C_{\text{pilot}})=\textsf{cert\_frac}(C_{\text{shrink}})=\tfrac{11}{60}=0.18\overline{3}\]
on the three-seed confirmation --- \emph{identical to the last digit}. That is not a coincidence and not a tie. The v2 exploitation step was
\begin{verbatim}
weighted = sorted(drivers, key=-scale[x])
for t in range(n_add - explore):
    draw[weighted[t % len(weighted)]] += 1
\end{verbatim}
which depends on \textbf{the order of $\sigma$ and on nothing else}. Shrinkage toward a pooled variance,
\[\tilde\sigma_i=\sqrt{\tfrac12\hat\sigma_i^2+\tfrac12\overline{\hat\sigma^2}},\]
is \emph{monotone} in $\hat\sigma_i$, hence order-preserving. So $C_{\text{shrink}}$ and $C_{\text{pilot}}$ produced byte-identical allocations on all $60$ instances. \oldclaim{Pilot and shrink are two allocation policies compared under common random streams.} They were one policy run twice. \eclass{DEMOTED: the v2 shrink row is not evidence about shrinkage}

\section*{55. Repair: Neyman-greedy allocation makes magnitude matter}
v3 (\texttt{functional\_variance\_stopping\_v3\_weighted\_shrink\_exploration}) replaces the ordinal rule by the exact marginal reduction of $\sum_i \sigma_i^2/n_i$:
\[\text{choose}\quad \arg\max_i \ \frac{\sigma_i^2}{n_i\,(n_i+1)} .\]
Allocating one sample to cell $i$ changes $\sigma_i^2/n_i$ by exactly $-\sigma_i^2/[n_i(n_i+1)]$, so this is greedy-optimal for the variance objective and depends on \emph{magnitudes} and on the evolving $n_i$, not on rank. \textbf{Measured effect:} pilot and shrink allocations now differ on $60/60$ instances (v2: $0/60$).

\section*{56. DEMOTION: the anytime policy was starved by construction}
In v2 the $20\%$ exploration block sat \emph{inside} the \texttt{\{C\_pilot, C\_shrink, C\_split\}} branch. The anytime branch fell through to driver-only selection with no exploration at all, so a cell outside the driver set could remain at its initial count forever; the confirmation showed $36/60$ runs with cells at exactly two samples and correspondingly enormous intervals. \oldclaim{$C_{\text{anytime}}$ underperforms because anytime confidence sequences are conservative.} The measured cause was starvation, not conservatism. \eclass{DEMOTED: the v2 anytime row measured a bug}

v3 hoists the exploration block \emph{above} the branch, so every adaptive policy reserves $\ge20\%$ of each batch for least-sampled cells. \textbf{Measured effect:} \texttt{cells\_at\_initial\_count} $=0$ for all six variants; minimum cell counts now $44$--$546$.

The forced exploration floor rescues the starved policy and \textbf{costs the policies that were already well targeted}, the oracle most of all. That is the expected sign of an exploration constraint and it is worth stating as a finding rather than hiding it: a mandatory exploration fraction is not free.

\textbf{Scope, explicitly.} v2 and v3 are different protocol versions and the comparison above is paired on seeds and instances but \emph{not} controlled --- two changes ship together. It is indicative, not a controlled ablation of the exploration floor alone. \eclass{NUMERICAL EVIDENCE, three seeds, uncontrolled pairing}

The regression suite for the two repairs (23 tests: shrink changes allocation under an order-preserving transform; no starvation for any variant at batch sizes $\{1,4,16,31\}$; diagnostics present) lives in \texttt{tests/}.
