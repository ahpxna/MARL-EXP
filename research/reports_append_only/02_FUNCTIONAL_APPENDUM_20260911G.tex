\textbf{Append-only rule.} Sections 1--76 are an immutable prefix. This addendum begins at Section 77.

\section*{77. The headline ratio is a point estimate, not an identified coefficient}
Section 74 reported $2.670\times$ against \texttt{uniform} and noted that \texttt{uniform} clears the censoring line by only $4.33$ percentage points. That caveat was directionally right but not quantified, and quantifying it changes what may be claimed.

A distribution-free $95\%$ confidence interval for a median at $n=300$ runs between order statistics $X_{(133)}$ and $X_{(168)}$:

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
variant & median & CI lower & CI upper \\
\midrule
\texttt{C\_anytime}     & $34\,788$ & $27\,068$ & $52\,188$ \\
\texttt{C\_known\_sigma} & $34\,532$ & $26\,732$ & $51\,596$ \\
\texttt{C\_pilot}       & $45\,957$ & $38\,029$ & $65\,069$ \\
\texttt{C\_shrink}      & $46\,613$ & $37\,405$ & $67\,117$ \\
\texttt{C\_split}       & $74\,817$ & $60\,473$ & $89\,289$ \\
\texttt{uniform}        & $92\,876$ & $79\,164$ & \textbf{censored} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Only $163$ of \texttt{uniform}'s $300$ runs certified, so $X_{(168)}$ falls beyond the horizon: its upper confidence limit exceeds $131\,072$ and is not identified. Five variants have fully identified intervals; \texttt{uniform} does not. Consequently the ratio $2.670$ has an identified \emph{lower} confidence side and an unidentified upper one, and must be reported as a measured point estimate rather than as a population coefficient. Dividing the marginal interval endpoints gives a valid but very weak one-sided bound, $79\,164/52\,188 = 1.52\times$.

\section*{78. The paired ratio is identified, and it is the statement to use}
The design uses common random streams (\texttt{common\_random\_streams = true}), so the variants are matched instance by instance and the paired comparison is both valid and far tighter than dividing marginal intervals. On the $163$ instances where both certify:

\begin{center}\footnotesize
median paired ratio $= \mathbf{2.183}$, \quad $95\%$ CI $= [\,2.067,\ 2.324\,]$
\end{center}

with minimum paired ratio $1.111$ -- \texttt{C\_anytime} is faster on \emph{every one} of the $163$ -- interquartile range $[1.761,\ 2.603]$, and maximum $3.859$.

Two features make this the right vehicle. It is free of censoring, because both members of every pair certified. And the instances it drops are $49$ where \texttt{C\_anytime} certified and \texttt{uniform} did not, against $0$ in the other direction: the selection removes exactly the cases where \texttt{uniform} performed worst, so the interval is biased \textbf{against} the conclusion it supports.
\claimbox{The revised reading of Sections 74--75. Identified: a paired median speed-up of $2.18\times$, $95\%$ CI $[2.07,\ 2.32]$, conservative by construction. Measured but not identified: the marginal median ratio $2.670\times$. Unchanged and not dependent on either: the sweep itself --- $163$ of $163$ paired wins, sign test $p \approx 1.7\times10^{-49}$, plus $49$ instances certified by \texttt{C\_anytime} alone --- and \texttt{false\_safe\_count} $=0$ across all $1\,800$ runs.}

\section*{79. The horizon comparison is still not available}
Section 76 recorded that the $65\,536$ probe used seed $8301$ and cannot be differenced against seeds $8201$--$8203$. The matched re-run at $131\,072$ on seed $8301$ has been launched twice: the first attempt lost its process before writing output and left an empty log, and the relaunch under a managed session has not yet produced a result file. Until it does, no horizon effect is claimed.
