\section*{61. Two lines of attack turn out to be one object}
Two independent efforts converged this week from opposite directions on the same quantity, and neither had named it:
\[R_m(\mu)=\max\{\,2D:\ \delta_\square\le1,\ \text{Top-C margin}\ \ge\mu\,\}.\]
One line supplied the \textbf{margin axis} --- regret as a function of required selection strictness, with the first frontier point $R_6(1/2)=9/2$ and a matching dual certificate. The other supplied the \textbf{symmetry axis} --- a block ansatz reducing the search over $2^{2^m}$ worlds to a $4k^2$-variable linear program. Composed, they make $R_m(\mu)$ computable in seconds for any $m,\mu$.

\claimbox{This is the first cross-chain object in the project that is \emph{computed} rather than searched. Every previous D6 instrument --- countersearch, exact LP ascent, tangent-cone seeding, tie-locus LP, face enumeration --- either failed or returned a demonstrated false negative on the same question. The lesson is recorded as a rule: \textbf{before building a search, check whether fixing the combinatorial data turns the question into a linear program.}}
