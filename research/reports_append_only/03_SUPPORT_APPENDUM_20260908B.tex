\section*{62. NOVELTY RELOCATION: this chain is adjacent to decision-region determination}
Addendum A, Section 59, closed by naming the Helly-shaped instance as the novel content and the equivalence as routine. A prior-art check now makes a stronger statement, and it must be recorded because it narrows what this chain may claim.

The question ``which queries suffice for a controller that is safe on every world consistent with the observation'' is the \emph{decision-region determination} problem. The characterisation
\[\bigl(\exists\,\text{controller}\bigr)\iff\textsf{FibreSafe}(Q)\]
is the standard observation that a decision rule exists exactly when each observation fibre admits a common safe action, and its proof here is \texttt{choose} applied to the existential. \textbf{It is not novel and this report does not claim it is.}

\claimbox{What survives, and what the chain should claim: a concrete instance \emph{inside this project's own abstract regret API} on which pairwise separation holds and whole-fibre sufficiency fails --- $\textit{bad}:\mathcal{P}(\textsf{Fin }2)\to\textsf{Fin }3$ surjective, so $\emptyset$ separates while no single decision serves the fibre. Its value is that it fixes the scope of \texttt{SUP\_B2} inside the corpus, and that the scope check of Section 59 found the corpus never relied on the false direction.}

\textbf{Consequence for the standalone paper.} The contribution is a scope result about this chain's certificate hierarchy, not a new theorem about query sufficiency in general. Any framing that presents the equivalence as the contribution should be revised before submission.
