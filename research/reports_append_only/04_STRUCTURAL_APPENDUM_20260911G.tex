\textbf{Append-only rule.} Sections 1--83 are an immutable prefix. This addendum begins at Section 84.

\section*{84. The $\chi \ge 4$ witness, promoted}
\texttt{LeanStructuralActualChi4KernelV1} re-proves the four subset enumerations of \texttt{LeanStructuralActualChi4WitnessV1} by kernel \texttt{decide} and repeats the derivation that consumes them, ending in \texttt{STRUCT\_ACTUAL\_CHI4\_prefix\_cover\_not\_at\_most\_three\_kernel}. Nine names, all on $[\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}]$.

Two things made this cheap, and both are worth recording because they are the reason the same trick will not always work. First, the four facts are \emph{pure combinatorics on} \texttt{Finset (Fin 6)} -- they mention neither \texttt{chi4F} nor \texttt{selectedRadius}, so no real-number reasoning had to be redone. Second, every other step of the original was already kernel \texttt{decide}, \texttt{norm\_num} or \texttt{omega}. The kernel load is bounded by $64 \times 20 = 1280$ decidable equalities on subsets of a six-element type.

The real-valued content is untouched: the original's \texttt{@[simp]} radius evaluations are reused through the simp set. They are \texttt{private}, so they cannot be named from the new module -- but a \texttt{private} theorem's \texttt{simp} attribute is global, which is what makes the reuse possible at all.

\section*{86. What the kernel replacements did not settle}
Each twin proves the same statement about the same instance. None of them widens the result: $\chi_{\text{prefix}} \ge 5$ at $m=7$ and $\chi \ge 4$ at $m=6$ are still statements about two specific integer instances found by search, not about a family. The negative finding of Section 73 stands -- staircases and disjoint unions have exactly two levels, so these witnesses are extreme draws rather than members of a structured family, and no construction is known that produces $\chi_{\text{prefix}} \ge k$ on demand.
