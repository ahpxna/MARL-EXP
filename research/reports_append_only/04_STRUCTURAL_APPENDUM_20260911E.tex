\section*{78. The tolerance law, both directions}
\texttt{unbalanced\_upper} gives, for any $c_i\ge\mathrm{osc}(f_i)/2$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
\[\chi_\varepsilon\big(\mathrm{selectedRadius}\,f\big)\ \le\ \Big\lfloor\tfrac{2\sum_i c_i}{\varepsilon}\Big\rfloor+1,\]
with \textbf{no balance and no pre-existing order} on the radii --- the ordering is removed by an explicit reindexing bridge. \texttt{intrinsic\_upper} specialises $c_i$ to the component radius, and \texttt{normalized\_upper} then gives $\lfloor2/\varepsilon\rfloor+1$ after normalising the total radius. In the other direction \texttt{normalized\_lower} gives $\chi_\varepsilon\ge r$ for the staircase at $\varepsilon\le1/(4r)$, and \texttt{one\_ranking\_half} shows one ranking suffices at $\varepsilon\ge1/(2r)$.
\claimbox{Upper and lower are now the same order in $1/\varepsilon$. Section 73 records that the lower bound is loose by exactly a factor two against the exact collapse point, and Sections 72 and 74 record that this family has no intermediate levels and that curve richness tracks coefficient precision. Those findings qualify what the pair of bounds means; they do not weaken the bounds themselves.}
