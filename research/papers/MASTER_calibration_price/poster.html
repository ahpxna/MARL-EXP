Objectives

A population of K·m items is split into K groups. Each group carries an unknown common offset. The answer wanted is a threshold set: which items lie above a fixed level.

An ordinary query reads one item, but sees its value and its group offset only in combination. A calibration query reads the group offset directly, at price κ relative to an ordinary query.

  • Decide when the calibration query stops being an efficiency gain and becomes mandatory.
  • Compute what it costs — the exact optimal value under a unit budget, not an order of magnitude.
  • Keep the novelty boundary honest: the design machinery this uses is decades old.

Framework

Write a = x/v for ordinary precision bought on an item and b = z/ν for control precision on its group; Δ is the separation of the two worlds to be told apart.

Γ* = Δ2 ⁄ [ 2K (√m + √κ)2 ] proved as a lower bound, an upper bound over every unit-budget design, and attained

Two readings follow immediately and are the practical content:

  • κ → 0. Γ* → Δ²/(2Km): free calibration recovers the cost of the ordinary queries alone.
  • κ large. Γ* ≍ Δ²/(2Kκ): calibration dominates the budget. The crossover sits at κ = m, where the two square roots are equal and each query type takes half the group's budget.

What is not new here

Read at theorem level, not from abstracts.

Dette & Titoff (2009), Thm 2.1: the T-optimal value equals the squared Chebyshev approximation error; Thm 2.2 characterises all T-optimal designs. Their criterion is already a profiled quantity, and the noncentrality is δ² = θ₀ᵀM₁₁.₂θ₀ — a Schur complement, the same shape as our I_u = D_u − M_u²/(2K).

Bennouna et al. (2025), Thm 1: a dataset is sufficient iff Δ(X,C) ⊆ span D. Every result is existential, over a predefined query set.

So profiling, the Schur shape, closed-form criterion values, and the identifiability fact are all cited, not claimed. Neither prior attaches a price to a query type, states a necessity dichotomy, or gives a rate in (K, m, κ).

Methodology

Step 1 — the dichotomy. Four items, two per group, exhibited in exact rationals:

θ⁽⁰⁾=(1, 0, 0, 1), b⁽⁰⁾=(0,0)
λ =(¼, −¾, 0, 1), b⁽¹⁾=(¾,0) hidden_shift_means — every ordinary query has the same mean in both worlds

hidden_shift_answers_differ shows the threshold sets differ. Item 0 is above threshold in the first world and below in the second; item 3 is above in both. So on such a group Γ = 0: no amount of ordinary sampling separates them.

Step 2 — profile the offset. profiled_information proves both halves: the inequality for every shift t, and the value at the minimiser.

inft [ a(Δ−t)²⁄2 + b t²⁄2 ] = abΔ² ⁄ [2(a+b)]

Step 3 — price one group. group_cost_lower is pure Cauchy–Schwarz on (s_m x − s_κ z)²: no optimiser, no multiplier theorem. group_cost_attained shows the split z/x = √(m/κ) meets the information constraint with equality.

Step 4 — sum over K groups. calibration_rate_upper quantifies over every unit-budget design, not a parametric family; calibration_rate_attained closes it.

Conclusions

A calibration query is not an efficiency improvement that a good allocator could do without. There are configurations on which every ordinary query has identical means in two worlds with different answers, so the value is exactly zero until a query of the new type is bought.

Once bought, its price is exact: the constant 1/(2K(√m+√κ)²) is matched by a lower bound, an upper bound over all unit-budget designs, and an attaining design — an identity, not a rate.

The surviving contribution is narrow by construction. Optimal discrimination design, nuisance profiling, the Schur-complement information and the sufficiency characterisation all belong to prior work. What is new is the price and the necessity, and the poster says so on the front.

Scope — what is not established

  1. The law assumes a common variance v, common control variance ν, common price κ, equal gaps. Heterogeneous cases are not covered.
  2. Figure 3 certifies one instance. It proves anchor-free designs can be strictly beaten, not that they always are.
  3. Figure 2's witness exhibits two indistinguishable worlds; it does not characterise all such configurations.
  4. Everything is an information/budget statement. No estimator and no finite-sample guarantee is claimed.
