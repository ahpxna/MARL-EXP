## 2. Methodology mined (what could help, and escape routes)

1. **Fixed-point / repeated calibration** (anticipatory guidance; performative RRM).
   - With the adapter reducing the light's sensitivity, iterated refits may converge (RRM converges if sensitivity ε < strong convexity / smoothness).
   - Tonight one round without the adapter did not help (C-109). → PERF (queue 040) measures whether the adapter reduces the publication sensitivity. A future method could alternate refit and adapter-tune to a fixed point.
2. **Small-gain view** (Zames; Jiang–Teel–Praly; networks: Dashkovskiy–Rüffer–Wirth).
   - The loop forecast → fleet → light → target is stable / consistent if the product of gains is < 1.
   - The light adapter lowers the light's gain to the fleet's eco-approach. This gives the paper a principled reason why BOTH interfaces are needed.
3. **Blackwell comparison** (Blackwell 1953, Ann. Math. Statist. 24(2):265–272, [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematical-statistics/volume-24/issue-2/Equivalent-Comparisons-of-Experiments/10.1214/aoms/1177729032.short)).
   - Under eco-driving, halting counts become a garbling of the light's decision-relevant demand. The speed-bin adapter restores a more informative experiment for the frozen decision rule.
   - Caveat: Blackwell ordering guarantees value only for an OPTIMAL decision maker; the frozen light is not optimal, so this is motivation, not a theorem about RL-H.
